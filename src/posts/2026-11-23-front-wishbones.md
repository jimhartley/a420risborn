title: Front wishbones
stage: front-end
---
Fitting the front wishbones was the first real step in the build. Until now I’ve been stripping things off the car and adding protection and things like that.

So, I started by finding all of the relevant components. The upper wishbone bushes were where you would expect, in the “Front Suspension” bag, but, there were other sleeves in there too. It dawned on me that the manual offers no guidance whatsoever regarding which to use where. Reviewing the [old 2015 manual](/documents/#build-manuals), it provides actual dimensions for the correct sleeves, giving confidence that you’re about to bolt on the right bit in the right place. I am clearly going to have to use the two references for the build. I think I will take the information from the new manual where it exists in both (for example where two different torque figures are provided), but take the info from the old reference where no information exists within the new version. On the subject of manuals, the printed copy that has arrived with my car isn’t even the latest version!

The upper wishbones were pretty straightforward. First, put plenty of protection around the work area. I use a lot of masking tape for this and if needs be, duct tape for extra toughness. If you do add duct tape, apply masking tape to the bodywork first and the duct tape on top of that, otherwise you might find your lovely new paint comes off with the duct tape. Next, dry fit the bolts through their mounting holes to check that the holes are clean. A couple of mine needed cleaning up with an appropriately sized drill bit. Now, with copper slip applied to the sleeve and bolts, offer up the wishbone and push the bolts in from the front of the car towards the rear. The longer bolt (65mm) is for the back and the shorter (60mm) for the front. The nuts are probably the trickiest little part of the upper wishbones, particularly the front one where the freshly installed IVA trim prevents it from spinning. Just hold it steady with a spanner and turn the bolt with a socket. That said, the front bolt needs to come back out to fit the headlight bracket so you could just leave the nut off altogether for now. At this point, I didn’t torque anything.

The bigger lower wishbones were a little more challenging, at least the first one was. Once I had worked out a way to get the washers in place, they were really not bad at all. There appear to be many opinions around the best way to get the washers in. This is what I did (after several failed attempts using other techniques), and it worked for me. Remember to always make sure that the chamfered side of the washers face the wishbone, and to use copper grease where shown in the manual.

1. Loosely attach the front arm without the two spacer washers, you can pop the split washer and single washer onto the bolt.
2. Hold the rear arm in place with a dowel, pencil, punch, anything that slips through the fixing holes.
3. Add the two washers at the front of the rear arm by slipping them into the correct location on the dowel.

{% image "images/front-end/lower-wishbone-washers-step-1.png", "Diagram of the lower wishbone below the chassis: a temporary dowel passes through the rear mounting, two washers sit at the front of the rear arm, and the front arm has its bolt, split washer and washer ready", "Steps 1 to 3: front arm loosely bolted, temporary dowel through the rear mount, first two washers on the dowel." %}

4. Remove the bolt from the front arm and fit the two washers onto the bolt. Refit the bolt and nip it up (don’t torque it, just remove any play).

{% image "images/front-end/lower-wishbone-washers-step-2.png", "The same diagram with the two spacer washers now added between the front arm's bush and the chassis", "Step 4: the two spacer washers added to the front arm." %}

5. Put the remaining two washers (the rearmost two) in place. One will drop in fine and the other may need a light tap with a hammer and punch (or screwdriver I guess but my punches were perfect). Once they are in position, the dowel or punch that is still holding the first two washers and the rear wishbone arm in place can be pushed through towards the rear of the car and used to align everything.
6. Finally, the bolt should go through from the back, towards the front.

{% image "images/front-end/lower-wishbone-washers-step-3.png", "The same diagram with the last two washers behind the rear arm, the temporary dowel pushed out towards the rear, and the bolt going in from the back with a nut at the front", "Steps 5 and 6: rear washers in, dowel pushed through to align everything, bolt in from the back." %}

At this stage I started having some doubts, or rather, doubts that I have had for some time crept back into my head. There are conflicting views about whether the wishbone bolts should be torqued now or not. I just can’t see how I will get a torque wrench in there once the car is on the ground. Anyway, I decided to trust the advice in the current manual which is contradictory to the older style manual.
