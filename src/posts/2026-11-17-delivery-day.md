Caterham have been excellent in getting to this point. Sure, the delivery is a couple of weeks later than I originally requested, but that’s okay. The key thing is, I had regular contact from Caterham, at least once the kit was in production. The delivery date had been booked two weeks ago and, at that time, there were six items missing from the kit. Critically, one of those parts was the bell housing and, without that, there would be no delivery. I thought this was going to be a problem until I got a very welcome email a few days ago saying that the bell housing had arrived, engineering had checked and fitted it, and, that the delivery would go ahead as planned. Exciting day! The van arrived early in the afternoon and between the driver and myself, we had it unloaded in no more than 30 minutes. My first look at the Sunset Riot paintwork didn’t disappoint, it’s a stunning deep colour.

{% video "videos/delivery/delivery-day.mp4", "Video of the Caterham kit being delivered and unloaded into the workshop", "Delivery day, in two minutes." %}

Two of us managed okay, perhaps a third person would have been useful. The Duratec engine came strapped to a plastic pallet. We carried it off the van and it slides around my Tuff-Tile floor without too much effort. No need for the dolly wheels that I thought I might need. The chassis was also relatively easy to manage but the driver explained that the only reason that two of us could manage it alone was that I had the mobile axle stands (CJ Autos). They are much easier than individual axle stands and I have to say, I am very impressed with them. They can be set pretty high (530mm if I remember correctly) but make sure your trolley jack can go high enough to help you get your car back off them one day! They also allow me to manoeuvre the car around the workshop easily, I’ve already found that really useful.

{% image "images/delivery/chassis-arrives.jpg", "The Sunset Riot chassis, still wearing its hood and side screens, being rolled into the workshop on red mobile axle stands", "Moving the car to the mobile axle stands" %}

After a good check of the paintwork and a few other bits, my first job was to get some of the bigger things out of the way. I have a decent workshop space but it will inevitably get overcrowded at times. With the help of a friend, we loaded two cars with the roll cage, roof, doors, bonnet, nosecone and rear wings (and later the seats), and took them down the road to a storage facility. There, they are safe and out of the way until they are required. The rest of the day was spent opening boxes, checking for damage, missing items (nothing found so far), and, labelling and putting components into the cabinets and onto shelves.

{% image "images/delivery/chassis-in-workshop.jpg", "The bare orange Caterham chassis with windscreen fitted, sitting on red mobile axle stands on the chequered workshop floor", "It’s all in and ready to get started" %}

Happy guy tonight, my project can really get started now.
