What should be a very enjoyable, simple job turned into a nightmare today.

First little job was to drill an 8mm hole which will be used to mount the horns later in the build. Drilling it after the steering rack is fitted doesn’t look very appealing. You can see in the image below that it sits just below the steering rack. I have popped the bolt in there ready to attach the horns later.

{% image "images/front-end/horn-mounting-bolt.webp", "Close-up of the Caterham-branded steering rack passing through the orange bodywork, with a small silver bolt fitted through the new horn mounting hole in the black chassis plate just below it", "The horn mounting bolt, just below the steering rack." %}

Now the steering rack.

All of the components were simple enough to locate and identify. The machined clamps are beautiful and the rack itself is too. The Universal Joint pushed easily onto the spline which was nice, and all of the bolts dropped nicely through the mounting holes without any cleaning up required. I protected the area well with plenty of tape and some cloths, then, fed the rack through the holes in the bodywork, starting with the passenger side and then back through the driver side when able. The clamps fit perfectly, both around the rack, and, the holes lined up correctly with the chassis so the bolts could just drop in. The washers and Nyloc nuts were a bit fiddly but no drama. The bolts should be left loose as the rack will need to be twisted into position later, then they can be tightened. The IVA boots slip onto the track rods easily once you have cut the closed end off with a sharp blade. Then spin the track rod end lock nuts on and the track rod ends. I wound them on 12 turns each but obviously they will be tweaked during setup.

So where did the nightmare occur? Securing the UJ! I have to hold my hand up and say that I am a novice, I’ve never fitted a steering rack before. I also have to say that the manual was less than helpful with no reference to the machined groove in the splines, either textually or pictorially. I pushed the UJ right onto the splines. The bolt then wouldn’t pass through the joint which confused me a little. I pulled the joint off the splines and the bolt passed straight through easily. Put the joint back on it wouldn’t. So, I went for the brute force option and started cranking the bolt in. I knew pretty much straightaway that this was wrong, but, by this time the bolt was stuck, the threads were stripped and nothing was moving anywhere. I tried all sorts to very carefully extract the bolt but it was stuck solid. In the end I had to cut the bolt and knock it out with a punch. I ordered a new bolt and UJ from Caterham and, now that I know that the machined groove is for the bolt to sit in, it all went together beautifully in seconds.

Frustratingly, I have since read the [old outdated manual](/documents/#build-manuals) which clearly tells you to note how the bolt should sit in the machined groove. Lesson learned.
