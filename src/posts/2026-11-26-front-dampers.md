---
title: Front dampers
stage: front-end
---
I don’t know why but fitting the dampers to the car is an exciting bit for me, just something that I’ve been looking forward to.

{% image "images/front-end/front-damper-fitted.jpg", "Side view of the front corner of the orange chassis with a black Bilstein coil-over damper fitted between the lower wishbone and the chassis, the bodywork and wishbone protected with blue masking tape and cardboard", "Front damper fitted, with plenty of protection still in place." %}

The dampers are smaller than the rear ones; obviously make sure you get the right ones. There are four identical sleeves in the front suspension bag, they are 8mm ID, 1/2” OD and 32mm long. A little copper grease and slip them into the upper and lower damper bushes. The bolts for the top and bottom attachments are in the chassis. Remove them and coat them with a little copper grease. There is a nick in the top ring of the damper that I positioned to the outside. Not sure why yet but the manual shows it that way so I went with it.

The top bolt goes in from front to back and needs a spring washer (AN) on the bolt first. I forgot this initially but quickly spotted my error. The big thing here is access. You will need to apply pressure with your thumb to literally bend the skin of the car which really does feel disconcerting. I was wondering what was going on with the paint beneath the masking tape but I have to say, it was fine when I uncovered it.

{% image "images/front-end/front-damper-top-bolt.jpg", "Close-up through the opening in the orange bodywork, showing a black cap-head bolt through the top damper mounting bracket, with blue masking tape protecting the paint below", "The top damper mounting: access is tight." %}

The lower bolt goes in from back to front. Both of the bolts can be torqued now, 25Nm. The red paint pen came out for the first time and marked the cap heads to show that they’ve been torqued.

That reminds me, I also have a spreadsheet where I record every fastener in each stage of the build. It tells me what the torque setting is but also if I’ve done it yet or not. I think it will be a very useful resource towards the end of the build when I’m going round torquing all of the fasteners that are not already done.
